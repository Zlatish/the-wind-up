Story of the week

Rexhep Rexhepi's RRCHF Chronograph Flyback wins the 2026 GPHG Aiguille d'Or

The Geneva-based independent took the Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève's top prize on Saturday for his hand-built flyback chronograph, beating the big groups. Other winners included Grand Seiko's Spring Drive U.F.A. Ushio 300 Diver for sports watches, Chopard's L.U.C 1860 for time-only and Daniel Roth's Extra Plat skeleton in the Essential category. For a UK dealer, the useful signal is reflected glory: GPHG wins tend to lift interest in a brand's more affordable models, so watch Grand Seiko and Chopard stock in the coming weeks. Monochrome

New releases
Omega Seamaster Diver 300M Ceramic (blue and brown)c. £7,500 and c. £11,650 (US $10,000 and $15,500)
Omega quietly added two 43.5 mm all-ceramic Divers, refs 210.92.44.20.03.001 in blue with titanium details and 210.62.44.20.13.001 in brown with Sedna gold, both on the no-date calibre 8806 with 300 m water resistance. Ceramic Seamasters have held value better than steel ones, so expect these to be sought after once UK boutiques have stock. The Gadgeteer
Yema Senans Grande Lune CMM.22c. £2,150 (€2,470)
Yema's first in-house moonphase is a slim 38.5 mm steel dress watch with a micro-rotor movement, a barleycorn guilloché dial and a sculpted luminous Moon. It is limited to 200 pieces per dial colour, which helps resale for a brand that usually trades well under list. Time+Tide
Serica Travel Chronometer Golden Green 8315-3c. £1,820 (€2,090)
The French microbrand adds an olive enamel dial and two-tone ceramic bezel to its 39.5 mm office-style GMT, with a COSC Soprod C125 and 200 m water resistance. It is a permanent model, so supply will not be scarce, but Serica has a loyal following and rarely appears second-hand in the UK. Monochrome
Breguet Classique Souscription in platinumc. £52,000 (CHF 58,000)
Last year's GPHG Aiguille d'Or winner returns in a 40 mm platinum case with the hand-wound calibre VS00 and 96 hours of reserve. Firmly a top-end piece, but it keeps Breguet's single-hand revival in the news. Monochrome

Did you know?

The tourbillon was built to beat gravity in pockets

Abraham-Louis Breguet patented the tourbillon on 26 June 1801. Pocket watches sat upright for hours, so gravity pulled the balance and hairspring the same way and caused steady timing errors. Breguet put the whole escapement in a rotating cage, so those errors average out over each turn. On a wrist, which changes position constantly, horologists still debate whether it helps accuracy at all, yet it won its own GPHG category again this year. Wikipedia

Dealer's notebook

Prices are steady this week, so there is no pressure to buy ahead of a rise. Entry Swiss divers such as the Oris Aquis lose 40% or more on leaving the shop, and that gives a dealer room to buy well and still price under new. Keep an eye on Grand Seiko after its GPHG win, as enquiries often follow the headlines. Wait for the Geneva auction results before pricing any vintage Rolex or Patek.

Also this week
  • Industry. Ateliers Associés, led by William Massena and Louis Westphalen-Lemaitre, plans to raise millions to give up to four new independent watchmakers a year business incubator support. More small independents reaching market means more interesting, less obvious stock for UK dealers in a few years. Hodinkee
  • Industry. A new market report says Rolex, Audemars Piguet and Patek Philippe make up 62% of a pre-owned watch market worth $26.6bn (c. £20bn). The big three still dominate resale value, which is why most UK dealers lead with them and fill around them. NEWS.am STYLE
  • Industry. Fossil has launched a premium, Swiss-made 'Signature' CS-1 collection, unveiled in Jaipur. A fashion brand pushing upmarket shows how much demand sits just below the established Swiss names. aBlogtoWatch
  • Auctions. Phillips' Geneva Watch Auction XXIV runs over this weekend, led by a 1945 Patek Philippe 1436 steel split-seconds chronograph estimated at CHF 2m to 4m (c. £1.8m to £3.6m) and a 'John Player Special' Daytona 6241 at CHF 800,000 to 1.6m. Results are not out yet; we will cover them on Tuesday. Phillips
  • Auctions. TimeForWatch has revealed 26 unique pieces for its December charity auction. One-off charity pieces often sell well over estimate, and some later reappear on the pre-owned market. WatchPro
  • Trend watch. Independents are taking the top prizes. Rexhepi's Aiguille d'Or, plus category wins for Daniel Roth, Ferdinand Berthoud and Cleguer, keep the independent wave going. Expect higher prices for anything from these makers that comes up second-hand. Revolution
  • Trend watch. Wave dials are spreading beyond divers. Time+Tide's round-up and Omega's new ceramic Divers both show the textured dial is now a style choice, not just a tool-watch detail. Time+Tide
  • Coming up. Sotheby's Important Watches sale in Geneva, 8 to 9 November, is led by a unique c. 1914 A. Lange & Söhne pink gold clock watch (CHF 500,000 to 1m) and a yellow gold George Daniels Millennium (CHF 300,000 to 600,000). Its online Fine Watches sale closes on 10 November. Haute Time
  • Coming up. Christie's Rare Watches sale, including the Watches for ELA charity lots, is in Geneva on 9 November. It is led by Zinédine Zidane's first Cartier. Hodinkee
  • Coming up. Watches of Switzerland will host the first public exhibition of the 2026 GPHG winners in London. It is a chance to see the Rexhepi and Grand Seiko up close; check the retailer for dates. Hodinkee
