Top story

Phillips' Geneva sale makes CHF 92m, the first watch auction past $100m

Phillips in association with Bacs & Russo sold all 217 lots in Geneva for CHF 92m ($112.3m, c. £86m), more than double its May 2025 Geneva total, with 24 world auction records and 17 lots over CHF 1m. The top lot was an F.P. Journe platinum Chronomètre à Résonance "Régence" at CHF 8.68m (c. £8.1m). WatchPro reads it as volume rather than ever-higher trophy prices, which suggests deep demand across the collectable end rather than a few headline bids, a healthy backdrop for UK pre-owned stock. New releases
Ming 51.01 HaloCHF 10,900 (c. £10,100)
Ming's new dress watch is 37 mm across and 8 mm thick in crystallised grade 5 titanium with a rose gold PVD finish, running a Ming-customised La Joux-Perret manual-wind calibre with 90 hours of reserve. It is limited to 150 pieces, with orders opening today at 1pm UK time, and small Ming runs have tended to hold their value well on the secondary market. Monochrome
Garrick S8£59,500 ex VAT (steel)
Norfolk watchmaker Garrick's new S8 is a compact 39 mm by 8 mm skeletonised time-only piece with a new in-house hand-wound calibre and about 60 hours of reserve, in 904L steel or precious metal (£73,000 ex VAT). Only five will be made a year, to order, so it is one for collectors of British independents rather than dealer stock. Monochrome

Did you know?

Why so many dials write four as IIII

Roman-numeral clock and watch dials very often show four o'clock as IIII but nine as IX, a habit that goes back to early clocks such as the Wells Cathedral clock of the late 14th century. Nobody knows for certain why. Some point to legibility and balance, and one old theory says Romans avoided IV because it began IVPITER, Jupiter's name. It is no rule, though: Big Ben's dials use IV.
