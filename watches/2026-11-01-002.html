Story of the week

Zenith opens talks on cutting up to half its Le Locle workforce

Zenith, owned by LVMH, has begun formal negotiations with staff over about 100 jobs at its Le Locle headquarters, roughly half the workforce there, with around 100 more roles at risk internationally. It is also expected to trim its collections and close its own retail operations until 2029, with no final decision before mid-November. For a UK buyer, uncertainty like this tends to soften pre-owned Zenith prices in the short term, so it may be a window on Chronomaster and Defy stock, but expect fewer boutiques to service and support it. Worn & Wound, citing Revolution

New releases
Louis Erard x Vianney Halter Futur Antérieurc. £4,500 ex tax
The third Halter collaboration is the closest yet to his cult Antiqua: a 38 mm steel case with two Sellita SW100 movements, one for the time and one for small seconds. It comes in gold or silver, 178 of each, priced at CHF 4,950 before tax. Past Louis Erard x Halter pieces have traded above retail on the secondary market, so expect demand. Monochrome
Christopher Ward C63 Sealander YalosFrom £1,290
A dressier Sealander: a 39 mm, 9.9 mm thick case, COSC-certified Sellita SW300-1 and four stamped, textured dials. It is £1,495 on the bracelet. A British brand at a sensible price, though Christopher Ward pre-owned values typically sit well below retail. Time+Tide
Tissot PRX Quartz Chronographc. £390
The PRX finally gets a quartz chronograph, using the same ETA G10.212 as the MoonSwatch in a 41 mm steel case with a textured blue dial and integrated bracelet. At $525 it undercuts most of the field. A strong, cheap first-timer piece that should sell quickly second-hand. aBlogtoWatch
Citizen Series 8 GMT NB6092 Damascusc. £1,650
A limited edition of 1,300 with a Damascus steel centre case and links, forged carbon and a true GMT calibre 9054. It is listed at $2,200 or €1,495. Limited Series 8 pieces are still niche in the UK, so stock lightly. Monochrome

This week's restructuring news puts Zenith in the spotlight, and the white-dial Chronomaster Sport is its best-known modern watch: a 1/10th-second El Primero chronograph with 60 hours of reserve. Pre-owned examples already sit about a quarter under list, and any uncertainty could push them lower, which makes it one to watch closely rather than rush. Loupe

Dealer's notebook

Hold off on buying Zenith stock until the mid-November decision, then look for motivated sellers. Black Bay 58s at about 40% under list remain the easiest steel sports watch to turn over. Submariners are drifting up again, so buy only where the price already allows a margin. Limited collaborations like the Louis Erard x Halter are worth chasing at retail, not at a flipper's premium.
