Story of the week

An F.P. Journe Souscription makes €10m, the most expensive watch ever sold at auction in France

Tourbillon Souverain Souscription No. 7 of 20, a 38 mm platinum piece from the subscription series that funded Journe's start, sold for €10,062,600 with fees (c. £8.7m) at Bonhams Cornette de Saint Cyr in Paris on 19 November. The estimate was €700,000 to €1.2m. In 2020 Phillips sold No. 14 for CHF 1.4m, so the best early Journe has moved several times over in six years. For UK buyers the lesson is the same as Geneva's: provenance and earliness now drive Journe prices far more than the model itself. Luxe.net

New releases
Roger W. Smith Series 7 Annual Calendar Tourbillon
The Isle of Man watchmaker marks 25 years of his brand with an annual calendar tourbillon rooted in George Daniels' work. Expect tiny numbers; it is a reminder that British independent watchmaking now sits at the very top of the market. Hodinkee
Seiko Prospex Marinemaster Save the Ocean HBF003€3,800 (c. £3,300)
A green-dial, 900-piece boutique edition of the revived Marinemaster: 42.6 mm steel, 300 m and the 8L45 automatic with 72 hours of reserve. Same euro price as the standard HBF001, on sale from 1 December; limited Marinemasters have tended to hold value well second-hand. Monochrome
Nomos Noir series (Tangente, Ludwig, Tetra)from €1,940 (c. £1,700)
Six black-dial, hand-wound references on the DUW 4001, in steel or gold, with small 27.5 mm and 32.8 mm cases. Gold versions run to €9,800 (c. £8,500); the steel ones are the easy sell in a UK market that still likes compact Nomos. Monochrome
Norqain Independence Skeleton 40 mm Arctic Blue
A transparent blue skeleton dial and Norqain's first semi-integrated steel bracelet. For resale, a proper bracelet usually widens the pool of buyers more than a new dial colour does. Monochrome

Did you know?

Vacheron's cross started life inside the movement

Vacheron Constantin's Maltese cross logo was not borrowed from heraldry. It copies a small part of the old stop-work, a cross-shaped wheel fitted to the barrel to stop the mainspring being overwound and to keep its force even. The maison registered the cross as its trademark in 1880, and it now appears on crowns, clasps and dials, including today's Overseas. WorldTempus

Dealer's notebook

Record Journe prices do not filter down to everyday stock, but they keep collector attention on independents and neo-vintage. Monthly indices show Omega and Rolex sports models softening into the winter, which is a reasonable time to buy Speedmasters and GMT-Masters for spring resale. Limited editions with a clear story, such as the 900-piece Marinemaster, are worth watching for early resale demand. Check Hong Kong results this week before pricing any high-end stock.
